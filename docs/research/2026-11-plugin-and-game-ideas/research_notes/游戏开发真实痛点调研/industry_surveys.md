# Survey data on game developers' biggest pain points and bottlenecks (2023–2026)

Scope note: these notes are numbers-first. Every figure has an inline source. Most figures come from press coverage or the publisher's own blog/press release, not the full PDFs. When outlets disagree, both versions are given. Survey sample sizes and dates are stated where the source gives them. Survey-based figures describe the people who answered, not the whole industry. GDC samples are about 58% US and over 90% North America/Europe. The Google Cloud, Unity and a16z surveys come from vendors with a stake in AI adoption.

## 1. GDC State of the Game Industry (2024, 2025, 2026)

### Takeaway
GDC's surveys show worsening labor conditions (layoffs and job insecurity) and fast-souring attitudes toward GenAI. AI usage is flat at about 36% of individuals, while negative sentiment rose 18% → 30% → 52%. PC dominates as a target (80–83%), and Unreal overtook Unity in 2026 (42% vs 30%). Self-funding is common (56% in 2025). GDC does **not** ask a "top development bottleneck" question, so its data mostly covers issues tools cannot fix (layoffs, funding, unions, tariffs). One exception: the 2026 survey names discoverability as a platform-choice driver (43%).

### Cited Findings

**GDC 2024 (12th edition; fielded Oct 11–29, 2023; n > 3,000)**
- 35% of developers were affected by layoffs, either personally or within their team or company. 7% of all respondents were laid off personally, compared with 22% of QA developers. Business and finance staff were least affected (2%). 56% were worried their company could see layoffs in the next 12 months. — [Game Developer: GDC 2024 State of the Game Industry](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/gdc-2024-state-of-the-game-industry-devs-discuss-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more)
- 84% were somewhat or very concerned about the ethics of using GenAI. 12% had no ethical concerns. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/gdc-2024-state-of-the-game-industry-devs-discuss-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more)
- Main engine: Unreal 33% and Unity 33% (tied), proprietary engines 14%. About one-third had switched engines or considered switching in the past year, mostly because of Unity's pricing and policy changes. Almost half had not considered switching. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/gdc-2024-state-of-the-game-industry-devs-discuss-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more); [GamesBeat/press search summaries](https://gamesbeat.com/gdc-survey-exposes-game-dev-views-of-genai-and-layoffs/)
- In open-ended answers, 51% of those interested in switching named Godot. This is a secondary figure, not checked against the PDF. — [search summary of GDC 2024 coverage, e.g. Pocketgamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/5-takeaways-from-industry-reckoning-ai-ugc-web3-game-engines)
- 76% use Twitter/X for marketing. 97% of those who mentioned X in open responses were negative about it. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/gdc-2024-state-of-the-game-industry-devs-discuss-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more)
- Accessibility measures were in place at 48% of companies, up from 38%. — same source

**GDC 2025 (13th edition; n > 3,000; ±2% margin of error; 58% US; published Jan 21, 2025)**
- Layoffs: 11% were laid off in the past year. 41% were affected by layoffs, including 29% whose direct colleagues were let go and 18% who saw other teams cut. 4% lost their jobs because the studio closed. Narrative was the most affected role (19%), business/finance the least (6%). Reasons given: restructuring 22%, declining revenue 18%, market shifts 15%, no reason given 19%. — [Game Developer: GDC 2025 State of the Game Industry](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/gdc-2025-state-of-the-game-industry-devs-weigh-in-on-layoffs-ai-and-more)
- GenAI: 52% work at companies that have implemented GenAI, and 36% personally use it. 30% think GenAI is harming the industry, up from 18%, while 13% think it helps, down from 21%. Personal use is highest in business/finance (51%), production/team leadership (41%), and community/marketing/PR (39%). — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/gdc-2025-state-of-the-game-industry-devs-weigh-in-on-layoffs-ai-and-more). *Conflict:* [GameDevReports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry) gives "use AI tools: 52%". That appears to confuse the company-level and personal-use figures. Use 36% for personal use.
- 27% do not use GenAI and do not plan to. 51% of companies have an AI policy (78% at AAA). — [GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry)
- Platforms: PC 80% (up from 66%), mobile about 28–29%, web 16% (up from 10%). 74% are interested in PC. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/gdc-2025-state-of-the-game-industry-devs-weigh-in-on-layoffs-ai-and-more); [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/pc-development-has-skyrocketed-gdc-survey-finds-80-percent-of-developers-are-now-making-games-for-pc-more-than-double-the-number-working-on-ps5-or-xbox-games)
- Engines: Unity 32%, Unreal 32%, proprietary 13%. — [GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry)
- Studio size: 21% are solo developers, 18% are at studios of 500+. — same source
- Funding: 56% self-funded their game, while 28% used publisher or project financing. Grants, VC and co-dev were each 15%. 89% rated self-funding at least somewhat successful. Accelerators were rated "not successful at all" by 43%, VC by 32%, and crowdfunding by 31%. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/gdc-2025-state-of-the-game-industry-devs-weigh-in-on-layoffs-ai-and-more); [GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry)
- Live service: 16% are working on one (33% of AAA developers), 13% are interested, and 41–42% are not interested. — same sources
- Hours: 57% work 40 hours or less a week (down from 64%). 13% work 51+ hours (up from 8%). Overtime is mostly self-driven (67%); management pressure accounts for 12% and fear of consequences 14%. — [GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry)

**GDC 2026 (n > 2,300; published Jan 29, 2026)**
- Layoffs: 28% were laid off in the past two years (33% among US respondents). Half say their current or most recent employer had layoffs in the past 12 months; the GameDevReports summary gives 58%. Layoffs were reported by two-thirds of AAA respondents and one-third of indie respondents. 74% of students worry about job prospects. — [GDC official article](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/); [GameDevReports 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-fcf)
- 48% of those laid off had not found a new job. Layoff reasons: restructuring 43%, budget cuts 38%, market conditions 38%, project cancellations 32%. — [GameDevReports 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-fcf)
- GenAI use: 36% use it in their job. That is 30% at game studios versus 58% at publishers, support and marketing/PR firms, and 58% of business professionals. Tools: ChatGPT 74%, Gemini 37%, Copilot 22%. **Uses: research/brainstorming 81%, daily tasks (email/meetings) 47%, code assistance 47%, prototyping 35%.** — [GDC official](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/)
- GenAI sentiment: 52% say it is having a negative impact (30% a year earlier, 18% the year before), and 7% say positive (13% in 2025). Negative views are held by 64% of visual/technical artists, 63% of designers/narrative staff, and 59% of programmers. 78% of companies have at least a basic AI policy. — [GDC official](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/); [GameDevReports 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-fcf). *Caveat:* coverage notes that GDC itself warns against direct year-over-year comparison because the samples differ. — [search summary of Game Developer coverage](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/layoffs-ai-and-next-gen-what-2-600-game-professionals-told-gdc-this-year)
- Engines (primary): Unreal 42%, Unity 30%, proprietary 19%. Unreal is the primary engine at 59% of AA and 47% of AAA studios. **54% of developers at older indie studios still use Unity; 11% of developers at newer indie studios use Godot.** — [GDC official](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/); [GameDevReports 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-fcf)
- Platforms (most recent project): PC 83%, PS5 47%, Xbox Series 40%, Steam Deck 28%, Android/iOS 23%. For the next project, 40% are interested in Steam Deck and 39% in Switch 2. VR/AR is 8%. **Platform-choice drivers: audience access 78%, business-model success 44%, discoverability 43%.** — [GameDevReports 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-fcf); [GDC official](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/)
- **Player-acquisition channels: social media 65%, streamers 39%, paid ads 31%, events 30%, community platforms 28%, press/bloggers 25%.** — [GameDevReports 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-fcf)
- Business model: 74% buy-to-play and the rest F2P (22% of indie, 33% of AAA). — same source
- Funding: 35% self-funded and 20% publisher/project financing (GameDevReports summary). *Possible conflict:* Pocketgamer.biz headlined that "self-funding rises." The 2025 figure of 56% likely came from a differently worded question, so the two are not directly comparable. — [GameDevReports 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-fcf); [Pocketgamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/self-funding-rises-among-game-workers-as-layoffs-persist-and-union-interest-grows)
- Hours: 59% work at least 40 hours a week and 11% more than 50. The top overtime reason is "not enough time within 40h" (59%). — [GameDevReports 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-fcf)
- Unions (US): 82% support, 5% oppose. Tariffs: 38% say 2025 US tariffs hurt their business. — [GDC official](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/); [GameDevReports 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-fcf)

### Inferences
- Many of the most-cited GDC problems are things tools cannot fix: layoffs, funding scarcity, unions, tariffs, and immigration.
- What GDC does show that tools can address: (a) discoverability is a top-3 platform-choice driver (43%) and acquisition runs mostly through social media and streamers; (b) the AI uses developers accept are utilitarian (brainstorming 81%, code 47%, prototyping 35%), not final art; (c) PC/Steam is the main target (80–83%), with Steam Deck a rising optimization target (28%).
- Strong anti-GenAI sentiment among artists (64% negative) and designers (63%) suggests that pitching AI as "replace the artist" will meet resistance. Code assistance, prototyping, and engine/editor automation are safer to position.
- For an indie-focused product, the engine landscape is fragmented: Unity is still used by 54% of established indies, Godot is growing among new indies, and Unreal leads overall.

### Gaps
- GDC does not ask "what is your biggest development bottleneck" or how dev time is split (art/QA/porting). I found no GDC time-allocation figures.
- I did not read the full GDC PDFs. Indie-only cuts beyond the items above were not verified.
- GDC 2024's personal GenAI usage figure (commonly cited as 31%) was not confirmed in the sources I fetched.

## 2. Unity Gaming Reports (2024, 2025, 2026)

### Takeaway
Unity's reports point to longer launch cycles in 2023, then a shift to smaller projects and shorter prototyping. AI use centers on coding assistance (62%) and narrative (44%), and half of surveyed devs already use MCP servers to connect AI to the engine/editor. Unity's 2025 report named R&D/tech-exploration lag (21%) and finding a unique concept (18%) as top blockers. The Unity samples are small (n=300 in 2026) and Unity is a vendor.

### Cited Findings
- **2024 Unity Gaming Report:** 62% of studios use AI tools, especially for content creation and animation (also conceptualising, prototyping and worldbuilding). 71% of those say AI improved delivery and operations. — [Game Developer/Gamasutra: Unity 2024 report 62%](https://www.gamasutra.com/production/unity-2024-gaming-report-indicates-62-percent-of-devs-are-currently-using-ai-tools); [Pocketgamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/game-development-time-slowed-in-2023-despite-ai-tech-but-71-celebrate-its-strengths)
- **2024 report:** average time to launch rose from 218 days (2022) to 304 days (2023) despite AI adoption. — [Pocketgamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/game-development-time-slowed-in-2023-despite-ai-tech-but-71-celebrate-its-strengths) (secondary; not verified in the PDF)
- **2024 report:** barriers to AI adoption were lack of time (43%), lack of technical skills (24%), and unawareness of AI capabilities (20%). In 2023, mobile games with multiplayer had 40.2% higher MAU than single-player games. — secondary summary via [games.gg](https://games.gg/ru/news/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-2024-unity-gaming-report/) and [Unity news](https://unity.com/news/2024-unity-gaming-report-highlights-resilience). Unverified against the PDF.
- **2025 Unity Gaming Report (GDC 2025):** 79% feel positive about AI in games. The biggest blocker cited was R&D delays, with 21% naming long lag times exploring tech. 18% named finding a unique game concept. AI use for automated playtesting, moderation, and code improvement rose, while use to replace artists/animators/modellers stalled. — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/3d/video-game-design/gdc-2025-ai-isnt-the-creative-cure-all-that-some-hoped); [Unity blog 2025 report](https://unity.com/blog/2025-unity-gaming-report-launch)
- **2026 Unity Game Development Report (Cint survey of 300 devs in 2025 plus telemetry from about 5M Unity developers):**
  - Median Unity project dev time fell 77%, from 91 hours (Jan 2022) to 21 hours (Dec 2025). This is a telemetry metric, likely editor hours per project, not calendar time to ship.
  - 67% spend 3 months or less prototyping.
  - 52% prioritize smaller-scale projects as their main way to reduce risk.
  - AI is used for coding assistance (62%) and writing/narrative (44%). Top benefits: efficiency (73%) and better decisions (62%).
  - **50% use MCP servers. MCP use cases: engine/editor connectivity 90%, production/project management 74%.**
  - Mobile devs' top challenges: competition 33%, rising UA costs 30%, discoverability 17%.
  - Cross-play: 38% name a consistent UX across devices as their main obstacle. 83% support online multiplayer, 55% focus on 2–9 player sessions, and 72% prioritize cross-play.
  - 90% rely on platform analytics and 73% on industry research for decisions.
  - Among small teams, 74% target new platforms and 42% explore multiple genres.
  — [Unity blog: 2026 Unity Game Development Report](https://unity.com/blog/2026-unity-game-development-report-trends); [Wccftech coverage](https://wccftech.com/unity-2026-game-development-report-points-to-smaller-teams-making-games-in-less-time-with-ai/)

### Inferences
- The MCP data (50% use MCP; 90% of those for engine/editor connectivity) is the most direct evidence I found for demand for AI-to-engine plugins and tooling.
- Recurring tool-addressable pain points: tech R&D lag (21%), concept ideation (18%), cross-device UX consistency (38%), multiplayer/cross-play implementation (83% support MP), and prototyping speed.
- Mobile pain points such as UA cost (30%) and competition (33%) are market problems that tools can only partly address.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the team-size breakdowns or live-ops percentages in Unity reports.
- The 2024 "lack of time 43% / skills 24%" barrier figures are secondary only.

## 3. Steam data: release volume, revenue distribution, wishlists (discoverability evidence)

### Takeaway
Steam got about 14.5k new games in 2023, about 18.5–19k in 2024, and about 20k in 2025. The median game earns only a few hundred dollars: Gamalytic estimates $222 for 2024 and $249 for 2025 releases. About 40% of 2025 releases did not earn back the $100 Steam Direct fee, and nearly half had 10 or fewer reviews. Wishlist-to-sales conversion has roughly halved since 2020–22. Together these are the strongest quantitative evidence that discoverability and marketing are the top commercial problem for indies.

### Cited Findings
- **Release counts:**
  - 2023: 14,531 per SteamDB at the time. Later filtered SteamDB queries give 14,111–14,310. — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/steam-breaks-another-annual-record-launching-more-than-14000-games-in-2023); [GamingOnLinux Dec 2025](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/12/over-19000-games-have-released-on-steam-in-2025-with-nearly-half-seeing-fewer-than-10-reviews)
  - 2024: "nearly 19,000" (19,078 with games and apps filtered on SteamDB instant search); a later SteamDB query gives 18,558. — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/nearly-19-000-games-released-on-steam-in-2024); [GamingOnLinux Jan 2025](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/01/valve-saw-a-record-breaking-nearly-19-000-games-released-on-steam-in-2024); [GamingOnLinux Dec 2025](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/12/over-19000-games-have-released-on-steam-in-2025-with-nearly-half-seeing-fewer-than-10-reviews)
  - 2025 full year: VGInsights 20,282, GameDiscoverCo 20,558, Gamalytic 20,353, SteamDB 20,008. Growth was +11% year over year, the third-lowest growth rate since Steam Direct (2017), but the highest absolute count ever. — [How To Market A Game: How many games were released in 2025](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/01/08/how-many-games-were-released-in-2025/)
- **Review distribution (2025, SteamDB, snapshot Dec 12, 2025):** 9,269 of 19,008 games released so far had 10 or fewer reviews, about 48.8%. — [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/12/over-19000-games-have-released-on-steam-in-2025-with-nearly-half-seeing-fewer-than-10-reviews)
- **Revenue distribution (Gamalytic estimates; 2025 releases from Jan 1 to Oct 21, 2025; analysis by Artur Smiarowski):**
  - Median revenue $249 (2024: $222). Average $358,900. Total $4.7B (2024: $7.6B).
  - 66% earned under $1,000, 40% did not recoup the $100 fee, and 9.9% earned $50k or more.
  - Copies sold: under 100 for 47.5%; 100–1k for 28%; 1k–10k for 16%; 10k–50k for 5.4%; 50k–100k for 1.2%; over 100k for 1.8%.
  - *Caveat:* the data covers part of the year, so late-2025 releases had little time to earn, which pushes figures down. Gamalytic's numbers are algorithmic estimates, not Valve data.
  — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/analysts-report-drop-in-game-revenue-on-steam-despite-growing-number-of-releases); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/with-over-13-000-new-games-on-steam-this-year-so-far-over-a-third-of-them-havent-even-made-enough-to-break-even-on-valves-submission-fee)
- Only 8% of Steam games gross over $100k, and 40% make less than the listing fee (OpenCritic headline on the same data family). — [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/news/22399/only-8-of-steam-games-gross-over-100-thousand-40-make-less-than-steam-listing-fees)
- **Indie concentration (VGI, data to Sep 30, 2024):**
  - Indies had 58% of Steam unit sales.
  - 53% of indie revenue (Jan–Sep 2024) went to "Triple-I" titles, meaning those with over $50M revenue and 1M+ copies in their debut year.
  - VGI's tiers: Hobby (1–2 people; 2k–20k copies; about $50k), Small Teams (3–15 people; 20k–200k copies; about $1M), Middle Market (15–50 people), Triple-I (50+ people).
  — [GameWorldObserver on VGI report](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/10/16/indie-games-revenue-steam-vs-aaa-titles-vg-insights); [VGI Global Indie Games Market Report 2024 PDF](https://vginsights.com/assets/reports/VGI_Global_Indie_Games_Market_Report_2024.pdf)
- Indie games made about 25% of Steam revenue in 2025 (Alinea Analytics, about $4.5B), and only about 300 indie games earned more than $1M. — [GameWorldObserver Dec 2025](https://gameworldobserver.com/2025/12/22/indie-projects-generated-a-quarter-of-the-total-game-revenue-on-steam-by-the-end-of-2025-analytics) (secondary; the 25% here conflicts with VGI's "48% of revenue" framing for 2024, because the methodology and the definition of "indie" differ)
- **Wishlist conversion (GameDiscoverCo, Nov 2025):**
  - Median first-week sales per wishlist is 0.15× for games with more than 25k wishlists at launch, 0.10× for games priced over $10, and about 0.14× excluding NSFW.
  - Underperformers spent longer on Steam before release (411 days on average vs 214 days for outperformers).
  - Underperformers had a lower first-7-day user score (67% vs 91%).
  — [GameDevReports summary of GameDiscoverCo](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-the-state-of-steam)
- An earlier GameDiscoverCo survey found a median of about 0.2 first-week sales per wishlist. — [GameDiscoverCo newsletter](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/steam-the-new-wishlists-to-first)
- **Wishlist inflation (Zukowski, secondary report):**
  - First-month sales per wishlist were about 0.45 for 2020–22 releases and about 0.22 for 2023–25 releases.
  - Launch wishlists still predict first-month sales well (R² 0.77 for 2020–22 vs 0.72 for 2023–25).
  - In Zukowski's Feb 2026 Next Fest data, wishlists held before the fest were the strongest predictor of wishlists gained (Spearman 0.825).
  - I did not verify this on howtomarketagame.com directly.
  — [Inven Global on wishlist inflation](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/27273/steam-wishlist-inflation-value-of-1-wishlist-cut-in-half-but-predictive-power-remains-unchanged)
- Steam visibility thresholds Zukowski cites: about 7,000 wishlists to enter "Popular Upcoming" and 10 reviews for review-score display and algorithm visibility. Typical dev time is 3–4 years for larger games. — [How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/01/08/how-many-games-were-released-in-2025/)
- Marketing survey (Bastion G2M Video Games Marketing Survey 2025; n=75 developers and publishers): discoverability is the biggest challenge. Coverage is internally inconsistent: 31% said it was the biggest challenge last year, while 94.4% expect it to be the biggest over the next 12 months or said it was the biggest last year. Unverified. — [JikGuard/press summary](https://www.jikguard.com/news/discoverability-is-the-industrys-achilles-heel-marketing-survey-finds)

### Inferences
- Discoverability is the main commercial bottleneck: about 20k releases a year, a median of about $250, about half with 10 or fewer reviews, and per-wishlist conversion roughly halved since 2020–22. The market is winner-take-most; Triple-I titles take 53% of indie revenue.
- Tools can address parts of this: Steam page and capsule/trailer optimization, a wishlist and Next Fest playbook, launch-timing and price analytics, press/streamer outreach automation, demo builds, and Steam Deck verification.
- Tools cannot address the core cause, which is oversupply and a game failing to stand out. GameDiscoverCo data suggests launch quality (first-week review score 91% vs 67%) and shorter pre-release periods correlate with outperforming. That argues for tools that help ship polished games faster and test hooks early, such as fast prototyping and playtest feedback.

### Gaps
- I could not verify Zukowski's bucketed launch benchmarks (for example "median indie converts about 12% on day one") on his own site. They are omitted as unreliable.
- There is no reliable public figure for the share of games that never recoup *development* costs (as opposed to the $100 fee). Developer budgets are not public, so Steam revenue estimates only bound this.
- I did not obtain VGI's median copies-sold figure.

## 4. Other surveys (Google Cloud/Harris, a16z, IGDA DSS, others)

### Takeaway
Vendor surveys report high AI adoption: 90% (Google Cloud) and 73% (a16z, small teams). That contrasts with GDC's 36% personal-use figure, because populations and question wording differ. The pain points they name (repetitive tasks, playtesting/balancing, localization, code, model quality) are tool-addressable. IGDA's DSS shows crunch at 28% (2023), and no newer DSS exists.

### Cited Findings
- **Google Cloud / The Harris Poll** (online, Jun 20–Jul 9, 2025; n=615 game developers in the US, South Korea, Norway, Finland and Sweden; released Aug 18, 2025):
  - Adoption: 90% use AI in workflows, 97% say GenAI is reshaping the industry, and 87% use AI agents.
  - AI uses (task-level figures as stated in the release): reducing repetitive tasks 95%, **playtesting and balancing 47%, localization/translation 45%, code generation/scripting 44%**, dynamic level design/animation/dialogue 36%.
  - Agent uses: content optimization 44%, dynamic balancing 38%, procedural world generation 37%, content moderation 37%.
  - Concerns: data ownership 63%, player data privacy 35%, unclear ownership of AI output 32%, AI integration cost 24%, upskilling 23%, difficulty measuring success 22%.
  - 29% say AI can help small indies compete, and 94% expect AI to cut dev costs long-term.
  - The release cites rising development costs, market saturation, and players sticking to older games as broader pain points, without percentages.
  — [Google Cloud press release](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2025-08-18-90-of-Games-Developers-Already-Using-AI-in-Workflows%2C-According-to-New-Google-Cloud-Research); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/87-percent-of-game-developers-are-already-using-ai-agents-and-over-a-third-use-ai-for-creative-elements-like-level-design-and-dialogue-according-to-a-new-google-survey); [report PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/global_ai_meets_the_games_industry.pdf)
- **a16z Games, AI in gaming 2024** (n=651; 84% on teams of fewer than 20 people):
  - Adoption: 73% of studios already use AI, 88% plan to, and 54% plan to work with their own models. By role, 85% of founders and 58% of artists use AI.
  - Results: 16% saw no productivity gain and 35% saw no cost reduction.
  - 3D assets: 70% use or plan to use AI for them, up from 48%.
  - **Main integration problem is model quality/accuracy (53%)**, followed by legal risk 12%, integration issues 11%, and team pushback 8%.
  - Job threat perceived by 36% of artists and 24% of designers/programmers.
  - Tools named: Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, Copilot, Meshy, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, ElevenLabs, Suno.
  — [GameDevReports summary of a16z](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/a16z-games-use-of-ai-in-gaming-in) (secondary; not verified against the a16z original)
- **IGDA Developer Satisfaction Survey 2023** (fielded May 17–Oct 20, 2023; n=777; released May 2024 with Western University):
  - 28% said their job involves crunch (33% in 2021), and 25% work long or extended hours not classed as crunch (22% in 2021).
  - Among those who crunch, 59% work 50+ hours a week and 10% work 80+.
  - Historical: crunch was 41% in 2019 and 51% in 2017.
  — [GamesPress: IGDA DSS 2023 release](https://www.gamespress.com/The-IGDA-and-Western-University-Release-2023-Developer-Satisfaction-Su); [IGDA DSS 2023 Summary PDF](https://xboxdev.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/IGSA-DSS-2023_SummaryReport_v5-1.pdf)
- IGDA paused the DSS: no 2025 edition, deferred two more years for financial reasons. — [GamesBeat IGDA interview 2025](https://gamesbeat.com/the-issues-game-developers-are-facing-in-2025-igda-interview)

### Inferences
- Across surveys, the most tool-addressable, AI-accepted tasks are code/scripting, playtesting/balancing, localization, prototyping, brainstorming, and repetitive-task automation.
- The biggest blocker to AI adoption is output quality (53%, a16z), ahead of legal or integration issues. Plugins should prioritize reliability and controllability over breadth.
- Data ownership and IP of AI output (63% / 32%) are trust concerns; a plugin that is clear about IP and data handling addresses them.
- Adoption rates disagree (90% Google vs 36% GDC) because of sampling (Google covers non-US markets, including Korea, and uses broader "any AI" wording) and vendor framing. Treat 36% (GDC) as the conservative Western individual-use baseline.

### Gaps
- **Time/cost breakdowns** (what share of dev time goes to art, QA/bug fixing, porting, or marketing): I found no recent survey with quantified figures. Only proxies exist: GDC 2024 QA layoffs at 22%, and Google's 47% using AI for playtesting.
- I did not research or verify the JetBrains game-dev survey, Stack Overflow Developer Survey game-dev slices, itch.io statistics, the Game Developer salary survey, or Ukie surveys.
- Unity 2025's full blocker list beyond the 21% and 18% figures was not verified.

## 5. Synthesis: tool-addressable vs not (for plugin prioritization)

### Takeaway
The most heavily evidenced problem is commercial: discoverability, marketing and oversupply. Tools can only partly help with that. The workflow pains with the clearest survey support are tech R&D lag, prototyping, coding, playtesting/balancing, localization, cross-platform/Steam Deck optimization, and multiplayer/cross-play. Funding, layoffs, and labor conditions are frequently cited but tools cannot address them.

### Cited Findings
- Discoverability and oversupply:
  - About 20k Steam releases in 2025 — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/01/08/how-many-games-were-released-in-2025/)
  - Median revenue $249 and 40% under $100 — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/analysts-report-drop-in-game-revenue-on-steam-despite-growing-number-of-releases)
  - About 49% with 10 or fewer reviews — [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2025/12/over-19000-games-have-released-on-steam-in-2025-with-nearly-half-seeing-fewer-than-10-reviews)
  - Discoverability is a platform-choice driver for 43% — [GameDevReports GDC 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-fcf)
  - Mobile devs cite discoverability (17%) and UA costs (30%) — [Unity 2026](https://unity.com/blog/2026-unity-game-development-report-trends)
- Workflow pains:
  - R&D lag (21%) and concept ideation (18%) — [Creative Bloq / Unity 2025](https://www.creativebloq.com/3d/video-game-design/gdc-2025-ai-isnt-the-creative-cure-all-that-some-hoped)
  - Cross-device UX (38%) — [Unity 2026](https://unity.com/blog/2026-unity-game-development-report-trends)
  - Playtesting/balancing (47%), localization (45%), and code (44%) as AI uses — [Google Cloud](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2025-08-18-90-of-Games-Developers-Already-Using-AI-in-Workflows%2C-According-to-New-Google-Cloud-Research)
  - Code assistance (47%) and prototyping (35%) — [GDC 2026](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/)
  - Demand for engine/editor MCP integration (50% use MCP; 90% of them for engine connectivity) — [Unity 2026](https://unity.com/blog/2026-unity-game-development-report-trends)
- Not tool-addressable:
  - Layoffs (28% laid off in two years) — [GDC 2026](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/)
  - Self-funding (56% in 2025) and poor results from VC and accelerators — [Game Developer GDC 2025](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/gdc-2025-state-of-the-game-industry-devs-weigh-in-on-layoffs-ai-and-more)
  - Tariffs (38%) and union and immigration issues — [GameDevReports GDC 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gdc-the-state-of-the-game-industry-fcf)

### Inferences
- Candidate plugin areas ranked by strength of evidence:
  1. Steam marketing and launch readiness (store page, capsule, trailer, wishlist and Next Fest planning, review-score risk). This targets the most-evidenced problem but can only raise the odds of success.
  2. Rapid prototyping and concept/hook validation (Unity's smaller-scope and 3-months-or-less prototyping trend; 18% concept struggle).
  3. Engine-connected coding assistant / MCP tooling (code at 44–62% across surveys; MCP engine connectivity 90%).
  4. Automated playtesting and balancing (47%).
  5. Localization (45%).
  6. Steam Deck / multi-platform optimization (28% develop for Deck; 40% interested).
  7. Multiplayer and cross-play scaffolding (83% support MP; 38% cite cross-device UX as an obstacle).
- Avoid positioning plugins around AI-generated final art. Artist and designer sentiment is 63–64% negative (GDC 2026), and Unity 2025 reports that "replace artists" use stalled.

### Gaps
- No survey I found quantifies the relative time cost of these workflow pains for indies. The ranking above is inferred from adoption and interest proxies, not measured hours.
